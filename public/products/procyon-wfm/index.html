Product · Procyon WFM

The rota is not a spreadsheet. It is a set of rules.

Procyon WFM is workforce management software for shift-based operations. It builds the week against the constraints that actually bind (rest periods, maximum hours, break entitlements and verified certifications), publishes one schedule the floor can rely on, then runs the shift: check-in, absences, replacements, overtime and the export that reaches payroll.

The problem it solves

The schedule is built in a spreadsheet, and broken by Monday.

A planner spends a day on next week's rota. Approved leave arrives late and blocks a published duty. A certification expired on Tuesday and nobody noticed. Someone does not turn up, so a shift lead rings round until a name is found. The overtime that follows is reconstructed from memory at the end of the month, and the reason it happened is already lost.

Procyon WFM treats the rules as part of the system rather than as knowledge in a planner's head. Hard constraints are enforced when the schedule is generated, so an unpublishable rota cannot be published. Where demand genuinely cannot be covered, the uncovered slots come back explained rather than silently dropped.

What the operation gets

  • A validated schedule, with one published version per week
  • Rest, hours, break and certification rules enforced, not remembered
  • Absences replaced from a ranked, eligible list in minutes
  • Overtime captured with its reason at the moment it happens
  • A payroll export that reconciles, and an audit trail behind every change

How it works

One week, start to close.

The same cycle every week: plan against the constraints, agree it, publish it once, run the shift, and close out into payroll and reporting.

  1. PlanInputs

    Production plan, approved leave, skills and certifications.

  2. PlanSimulate

    Scenarios scored, hard rules enforced, gaps explained.

  3. AgreeApprove

    A supervisor approves or returns it; shortages are justified.

  4. AgreePublish

    One version per week; crews are notified and acknowledge.

  5. RunRun the shift

    Check-in, breaks, absences, replacements and overtime.

  6. CloseClose and export

    Shift close, payroll export, reports and insight.

The improvement loop. What the week actually cost feeds the next week's plan. Every step is permissioned separately, and the person who prepares a schedule is never the person who approves it.

What is in it

Six working areas, one system.

Planning, the shift itself and the close all work from the same people, rules and records, so nothing is re-keyed between them.

The rules are the product

A schedule that cannot break the rules.

Rest periods, daily and weekly maximums, consecutive-day limits, break entitlement and certification validity are held as a versioned rule set, not as conventions. The planning engine enforces them while it builds, so a rota that would breach them is never offered.

Where demand cannot be covered inside the rules, the system says which slots are uncovered and why. A shortage can only be accepted deliberately, with a comment, by someone with the authority to accept it. Change the rules and a new version is created; published schedules keep the rules they were built under.

Built for the people who are not at a desk.

Operators check in from a phone or a shared kiosk, shift leads fill gaps from a ranked list, and supervisors see only what their role allows.

Why it holds up

The parts most rota tools leave out.

  • Separation of duties.The person who prepares a schedule cannot approve it, and an approver cannot approve their own work.
  • One published version.Exactly one schedule is live per week. Late changes are versioned and notified, never quietly overwritten.
  • Explained shortages.Uncovered demand is reported with the reason, so a gap is a decision somebody took rather than an accident.
  • Evidence, not inference.Exception patterns are presented as something to investigate. The system does not claim to know why.

Questions about Procyon WFM.

What is Procyon WFM?

Procyon WFM is workforce management software for shift-based operations, built by Procyon Technostructure. It plans the weekly rota against rest periods, maximum hours, break entitlements and verified certifications, publishes one schedule version per week, and then runs the shift: check-in, attendance, absences, ranked replacements, overtime and the payroll export.

How does Procyon WFM enforce rest periods and working-hour limits?

The rules are held as a versioned rule set and enforced by the planning engine while it builds the schedule, so a rota that would breach them is never offered. Out of the box the defaults are an 11-hour minimum rest, 12 hours per day, 48 hours per week, 6 consecutive days and a break after 6 hours. Every value is configurable per site.

What happens when demand cannot be covered within the rules?

The uncovered slots are reported with the reason, for example no rested, certified person is available. A shortage can only be accepted deliberately, with a comment, by someone with the authority to accept it, so every gap is a recorded decision rather than an accident.

How does Procyon WFM choose a replacement when someone is absent?

A vacancy opens as soon as the absence is confirmed. Candidates are ranked on eligibility, rest, skill and fairness, anyone with pending leave is excluded, and the ranking weights are visible. Vacancies that stay open too long escalate, and the filled duty is dated, attributed and auditable.

Can operators check in without a reliable network connection?

Yes. Operators can self check-in from a phone against the published duty, or use a shared kiosk with large buttons and a privacy timeout. The kiosk keeps an offline queue and replays it when the connection returns, and duplicate punches are detected rather than double counted.

How does overtime reach payroll?

Overtime is captured at the moment it happens against a reason code, approved by someone with the authority to approve it, rounded to the configured interval (15 minutes by default) and included in the payroll export at shift close, with an audit trail behind every change.

Does Procyon WFM support Arabic and multiple sites?

Yes. The application runs in English and Arabic, including right-to-left layout. Time zone, currency and week start are configured per site, rules can differ by site, and the application can carry the organization's own name, logo and colours.

Who is Procyon WFM for?

Operations that run shifts against a production or service plan, such as manufacturing plants, and the people who run them: planners, supervisors, shift leads and operators. Each role sees only its own screens, and the person who prepares a schedule is never the person who approves it.
