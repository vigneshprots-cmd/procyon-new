Our view

Most breaches use a gap the last audit already named.

Mid-market security programs rarely fail for lack of tools. They fail in the hours nobody is watching, in the accounts nobody closed, and in the weeks between a critical patch being released and being installed.

The fixes are mostly operational: coverage after hours, identity hygiene, patch latency tracked as a number, and compliance evidence that collects itself, so the audit becomes a report instead of a project. IBM put the average breach at $4.88M in 2024; closing these four gaps is far cheaper.

  • Alerts nobody reads after 6 p.m.

    A detection console that pages no one at night is a log, not a control. A working week covers 40 of the 168 hours, and attackers know which ones are left.First move: 24x7 triage with the authority to isolate an endpoint

  • MFA with exceptions

    MFA is on for most staff, but not for a legacy admin console, a service account or the old VPN, and accounts of people who left are still active. Attackers look for the exception, not the rule.First move: MFA on every admin role this week, then a leaver access review

  • Patch latency nobody measures

    Critical patches wait for a monthly change window, and nobody can say how many days the average one takes to land.First move: days-to-patch for critical CVEs, reported every month

  • Evidence gathered by hand

    Screenshots and spreadsheets assembled in the weeks before each audit by people who have other jobs, then thrown away until the next one.First move: map controls once, then automate the evidence
