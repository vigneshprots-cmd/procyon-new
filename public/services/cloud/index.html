Our view

Cloud bills climb when the plan starts with the vendor.

Most cloud programs that disappoint were planned as one decision: pick a provider, lift everything across, optimize later. Later rarely arrives. The bill rises, the project team leaves, and nobody can say which team owns which cost.

The work that pays back is less exciting and more specific: a decision for each workload, a landing zone every account inherits, and cost ownership set before the first wave moves, not reconstructed from invoices afterwards.

  • Lift and shift as the default

    Rehosting everything carries the old inefficiency with it: servers sized for a peak that comes twice a year, licenses that do not travel well, and test environments that run around the clock.First move: a 6Rs decision per workload, including retire and retain

  • Accounts before guardrails

    Teams opened accounts and subscriptions as they needed them, so identity, networking and logging differ everywhere, and security reviews never catch up with the drift.First move: a landing zone with SSO, MFA and policy guardrails that every account inherits

  • Spend with no owner

    Untagged resources, no budget per team, and savings plans never bought because nobody could forecast the base load with confidence.First move: tagging and team budgets in place before the first wave

  • Day 2 with nobody on call

    The migration finishes, the project team leaves, and incidents, capacity and cost at 2 a.m. belong to no one in particular.First move: agree the run model before cutover, not after it

Migration strategy

Six decisions, made one workload at a time.

The 6Rs are not a framework slide. They are the line items of the business case, and the mix between them decides most of the cost and most of the risk.

Rehost
Move as is. Right for a workload with a near-term exit date or a datacenter contract ending. Wrong as the default, because it carries every oversized server across.
Replatform
Small changes to use a managed service, such as a managed database in place of a self-run one. Often the best ratio of effort to saving.
Refactor
Re-architect for cloud-native services. Worth it for systems that change often and matter to revenue; expensive vanity for the rest.
Repurchase
Replace with SaaS. Common for CRM, HR and IT service management, where Salesforce or ServiceNow already does the job better than custom code.
Retire
Switch it off. Every portfolio carries applications nobody uses, and retiring them is the cheapest migration there is.
Retain
Leave it where it is for now, because of latency, licensing or a replacement already planned. A deliberate decision, written down, not a gap in the plan.

FinOps

Savings stick when a named team owns the number.

A one-off cleanup saves money for a quarter. The 30 to 40% reductions that hold come from four habits, built into the foundation rather than bolted on later.

  • Tagged at creationOwner, cost center and environment tags enforced by policy when a resource is created, so untagged spend is flagged the same day instead of found at month end.
  • Budgets per teamEach team sees its own spend against its own budget, with anomaly alerts that reach the person who can act on them.
  • Rightsize, then commitInstances are rightsized first and savings plans bought on the measured base load afterwards. In the other order, the commitment locks in the waste.
  • Unit economicsCost per business capability, such as per order or per customer, so a rising bill can be read as growth or as waste.

Asked before a cloud move.

AWS, Azure or Google Cloud: which is best?

Whichever best fits the workloads, skills and ISV ecosystem. Procyon is certified across all three and designs multi-cloud-ready landing zones. The first decision is workload by workload, not vendor first.

How are runaway cloud bills avoided?

FinOps is built in from day one: tagging, budget alerts, anomaly detection, rightsizing, savings plans and unit economics per business capability. Most clients see a 30 to 40% cost reduction within two quarters.

Can complex migrations happen with minimal downtime?

Yes. A 6Rs assessment classifies every workload, then wave-based execution, automated cutover and blue-green strategies keep downtime close to zero. More than 300 workloads have been moved, including SAP, Oracle DB and large data estates.

How is security handled across multiple clouds?

Through a unified landing zone with identity-first access, CSPM and CWPP across clouds, secrets management and centralized logging into the SIEM, so compliance is continuous.

What happens after the migration?

24x7 SRE-led managed cloud services cover incident response, FinOps, security posture, capacity planning and continuous modernization. Most clients move to managed run within 90 days of go-live.
