Procyon WFM

The rota is not a spreadsheet. It is a set of rules.

It builds the week against the constraints that actually bind (rest periods, maximum hours, break entitlements and verified certifications), publishes one schedule the floor can rely on, then runs the shift through to payroll.

The problem

The schedule is built in a spreadsheet, and broken by Monday.

A planner spends a day on next week's rota. By the time it reaches the floor, the rules it was meant to respect live only in that planner's head.

Leave that arrives late

Approved leave lands after the rota is out and blocks a duty that was already published.

Certifications that lapse unseen

A certification expired on Tuesday, and nobody noticed until someone was already on the line.

No-shows filled by phone

Someone does not turn up, so a shift lead rings round until a name is found.

Overtime rebuilt from memory

The overtime is reconstructed at month end, and the reason it happened is already lost.

What is Procyon WFM?

Rules held by the system, not in a planner's head.

Procyon WFM is workforce management software developed by Procyon Technostructure for operations that run shifts against a production or service plan. Hard constraints are enforced while the schedule is generated, so an unpublishable rota cannot be published.

Where demand genuinely cannot be covered, the uncovered slots come back explained rather than silently dropped, and a shortage can only be accepted deliberately, with a comment, by someone with the authority to accept it.

  • A validated schedule, with one published version per week
  • Rest, hours, break and certification rules enforced
  • Absences replaced from a ranked, eligible list in minutes
  • Overtime captured with its reason at the moment it happens
  • A payroll export that reconciles, with an audit trail behind every change

How Procyon WFM works

One week, start to close.

The same cycle every week: plan against the constraints, agree it, publish it once, run the shift, and close out into payroll and reporting.

The improvement loop. What the week actually cost feeds the next week's plan. Every step is permissioned separately, and the person who prepares a schedule is never the person who approves it.

The rules are the product

A schedule that cannot break the rules.

Rest periods, daily and weekly maximums, consecutive-day limits, break entitlement and certification validity are held as a versioned rule set. Change the rules and a new version is created; published schedules keep the rules they were built under.

Enforced while it builds

The planning engine applies the rule set as it generates the week, so a rota that would breach a rest period, an hours limit or a certification is never offered.

Shortages explained, never hidden

Where demand cannot be covered inside the rules, the system says which slots are uncovered and why, and a shortage is accepted only on purpose, with a comment.

Why it holds up

The parts most rota tools leave out.

Separation of duties

The person who prepares a schedule cannot approve it, and an approver cannot approve their own work.

One published version

Exactly one schedule is live per week. Late changes are versioned and notified, never quietly overwritten.

Explained shortages

Uncovered demand is reported with the reason, so a gap is a decision somebody took rather than an accident.

Evidence, not inference

Exception patterns are presented as something to investigate. The system does not claim to know why.

Frequently asked questions

Procyon WFM is workforce management software for shift-based operations, built by Procyon Technostructure. It plans the weekly rota against rest periods, maximum hours, break entitlements and verified certifications, publishes one schedule version per week, and then runs the shift: check-in, attendance, absences, ranked replacements, overtime and the payroll export.

The rules are held as a versioned rule set and enforced by the planning engine while it builds the schedule, so a rota that would breach them is never offered. Out of the box the defaults are an 11-hour minimum rest, 12 hours per day, 48 hours per week, 6 consecutive days and a break after 6 hours. Every value is configurable per site.

The uncovered slots are reported with the reason, for example no rested, certified person is available. A shortage can only be accepted deliberately, with a comment, by someone with the authority to accept it, so every gap is a recorded decision rather than an accident.

A vacancy opens as soon as the absence is confirmed. Candidates are ranked on eligibility, rest, skill and fairness, anyone with pending leave is excluded, and the ranking weights are visible. Vacancies that stay open too long escalate, and the filled duty is dated, attributed and auditable.

Yes. Operators can self check-in from a phone against the published duty, or use a shared kiosk with large buttons and a privacy timeout. The kiosk keeps an offline queue and replays it when the connection returns, and duplicate punches are detected rather than double counted.

Overtime is captured at the moment it happens against a reason code, approved by someone with the authority to approve it, rounded to the configured interval (15 minutes by default) and included in the payroll export at shift close, with an audit trail behind every change.

Yes. The application runs in English and Arabic, including right-to-left layout. Time zone, currency and week start are configured per site, rules can differ by site, and the application can carry the organization's own name, logo and colours.
