Salesforce AI Business Case: A Practical ROI Framework

Leadership will not fund a demo. This framework turns Salesforce AI ambition into a business case a CFO can approve.

The hardest part of a Salesforce AI project is often not the technology; it is getting it funded. Leadership will not sign off on a demo. They will sign off on a business case that names a value lever, a baseline, and a credible number. Here is how to build that case, which metrics actually persuade a CFO, and how to avoid the vanity ROI that gets a project cancelled at its first review.

THE NUMBER THAT SHOULD SHAPE YOUR CASE
Gartner projects that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 , most for unclear business value rather than technical failure. A rigorous, measurable business case is the single best protection against being in that 40%. Source: Gartner

Anchor the case to a value lever, not a feature

A business case built on 'we will use AI' fails. A business case built on 'we will cut average handle time in service by X, worth Y' gets funded. Every credible Salesforce AI case begins by naming the lever it moves and the number attached to it.

The four-step ROI framework

  1. Baseline the current state. Measure the metric today before anything changes. No baseline, no defensible ROI.

  2. Model a conservative improvement. Estimate the change, then halve your optimism. A conservative case that clears the bar beats an ambitious one that gets picked apart.

  3. Cost the whole effort honestly. Include implementation, data readiness, and change management, not just licensing. Under-costing is how projects blow their own ROI.

  4. Define the review checkpoint. Name when and how you will prove it worked. A case with a built-in measurement point survives scrutiny.

The metrics that actually move a CFO

Executives discount soft claims, so anchor the case in numbers they already trust:

  • Hard cost: reduction in cost to serve or hours of manual work removed.

  • Capacity: the same team handling more volume without hiring.

  • Speed: shorter cycle or resolution time, tied to revenue or retention.

  • Payback period: when the investment turns net positive - the number a CFO remembers.

One more discipline decides whether a funded case actually ships: feasibility. A high-value use case that is not feasible is a plan to spend money slowly. Score every candidate on value and feasibility - is the data ready, how complex is the build, how much change does it demand - so the case you fund is the one most likely to reach production. Our AI-powered business and data strategy teams exist precisely to make high-value cases feasible.

THE VANITY-ROI TRAP
The use case that draws the loudest applause is often the one with the least measurable value. Guard against it: make every candidate declare its owner and its metric before it competes for budget. Impressive is not the same as fundable.
