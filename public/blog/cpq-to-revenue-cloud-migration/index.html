CPQ to Revenue Cloud Migration: What to Expect

Salesforce CPQ went end of sale in 2025. Moving to Revenue Cloud is not an upgrade - it is a reimplementation, and planning matters.

If you run Salesforce CPQ, the ground has already shifted under you. CPQ went end of sale, and its successor is not an upgrade you install - it is a reimplementation you plan. Understanding what that really means is the difference between a controlled transition and a scramble.

THE CORE FACT

Salesforce CPQ is now a frozen, end-of-sale product - no new customers, no new features - and all new investment flows into Revenue Cloud Advanced. The catch that changes everything: the move is a reimplementation, not an upgrade. CPQ runs on managed-package custom objects; Revenue Cloud is built on standard platform objects, so pricing logic and bundles are rebuilt rather than copied across. Source: Sweep · Everstage

What Actually Changed

Salesforce CPQ has reached end of sale: no new customers, and no new feature development. Salesforce has not published a hard end-of-life date, so existing customers can keep using and renewing CPQ for now. But make no mistake - it is a frozen product. All new investment flows into Revenue Cloud Advanced (previously Revenue Lifecycle Management), rebuilt on the standard Salesforce platform. The strategic read: this is less a deadline to fear and more a forcing function to modernize quote-to-cash on your own terms.

Why It Is a Reimplementation, Not an Upgrade

This is the single most important thing to understand. Salesforce CPQ is a managed package built on custom objects. Revenue Cloud Advanced is built on standard platform objects. They are different foundations, so there is no one-click migration tool that lifts your configuration across.

Salesforce CPQ Revenue Cloud Advanced
Managed package Native platform
Custom objects Standard platform objects
Frozen (end of sale) Active roadmap and investment
Familiar to your admins New model and skills to learn

Your product catalog, pricing rules, discount logic, and bundle structures are rebuilt and re-tested against the new model, not copied. That is more work than an upgrade - but it is also a rare chance to retire years of accumulated CPQ complexity instead of carrying it forward.

What a Migration Involves

  1. Discovery and rationalization: inventory every product, rule, and bundle - and decide what still earns its place. Most CPQ orgs carry logic no one uses.

  2. Data cleanup: clean the product and pricing data before it moves. Debt migrated is debt multiplied.

  3. Rebuild on standard objects: model products, pricing, and bundles in Revenue Cloud Advanced using upgrade-safe patterns.

  4. Integration and quote-to-cash: reconnect CRM, ERP, billing, and any downstream systems.

  5. Test against real deals: validate pricing and bundles with real, messy quote scenarios, not just clean samples.

  6. Phased cutover and enablement: roll out with training so RevOps and sales adopt the new model.

THE MOST COMMON MISTAKE

Treating the move as a like-for-like rebuild. Teams that simply recreate their old CPQ configuration in the new platform inherit all of its complexity and miss the one opportunity a reimplementation offers: to simplify the pricing model itself.

Migrate Now or Wait?

If your CPQ works and you have no urgent driver, you are not forced to move today - but a reimplementation needs exactly what waiting consumes: time. The pragmatic path for most teams is to start discovery and data cleanup early so the build can happen on your schedule, with a rationalized catalog, rather than under pressure later. A CPQ-to-Revenue-Cloud move is a natural part of a broader application modernization plan, and it lives inside our Salesforce practice.
