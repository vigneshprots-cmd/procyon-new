Cloud Migration Strategy: The 6 R's Framework for US Enterprises

Not every workload should move to the cloud the same way. The 6 R's framework helps you decide, application by application, what to do and why.

The fastest way to a disappointing cloud program is to treat migration as a single decision. 'We are moving to the cloud' is not a strategy - it is a headline. The real work is deciding, application by application, how each workload should move, and being honest that some of them should not move at all yet. The 6 R's framework is the industry-standard way to make those calls consistently across a portfolio. Here is how each option works, when to use it, and how to avoid the trap of lifting and shifting everything and calling it transformation.

WHY THE APPROACH MATTERS

MuleSoft's 2026 benchmark shows 86% of leaders still fighting integration complexity - much of it created when workloads move to the cloud without a plan. A workload that was inefficient on-premises is simply inefficient in the cloud, now with a monthly bill attached. The 6 R's exist so you can be deliberate. Source: MuleSoft 2026

Why 'Lift and Shift Everything' Fails Quietly

Rehosting an entire estate to the cloud is fast, and it feels like progress. But as a whole-portfolio strategy it delivers a data center in someone else's building. Lift and shift is a legitimate tactic for parts of a portfolio; applied to everything, it just relocates your inefficiency. The 6 R's exist so you can decide deliberately instead.

The 6 R's, Explained

1
Rehost (lift and shift)

Move the workload as-is. Fast and low-risk. Best for stable applications, tight timelines, or a first step you will optimize later.

2
Replatform (lift and reshape)

Make targeted improvements during the move - a managed database, autoscaling, container packaging - without a full rewrite. Best when small changes unlock outsized operational gains.

3
Refactor (re-architect)

Rebuild the application to be cloud-native. Highest effort, highest payoff. Reserve it for high-value, high-change applications where scalability is a genuine differentiator.

4
Repurchase (move to SaaS)

Replace a custom or legacy application with a commercial subscription product. Best for commodity functions where a mature SaaS option does the job better.

5
Retire (decommission)

Turn it off. Most portfolios carry applications no one truly uses. Migration is the perfect moment to find and retire them.

6
Retain (keep for now)

Some workloads should stay on-premises due to compliance, latency, or dependencies. Retaining deliberately is a decision; retaining by neglect is not.

START WITH A PORTFOLIO ASSESSMENT

You cannot assign the 6 R's from intuition. Score every application on business value, technical complexity, and constraints such as compliance and dependencies. The assessment is what turns 'move to the cloud' into a defensible, sequenced plan.

Sequencing the Program

  1. Assess the portfolio and assign an R to every workload.

  2. Start with quick, low-risk wins (rehost and retire) to build momentum and free capacity.

  3. Replatform the operationally painful workloads next for fast operational relief.

  4. Refactor the strategic few deliberately, with clear success metrics.

  5. Establish landing-zone governance early - security, identity, and monitoring - so growth stays controlled.

THE MOST COMMON MISTAKE

Teams refactor the wrong applications first - usually the ones the loudest engineer finds interesting, not the ones the business depends on. Let the portfolio assessment, not enthusiasm, drive sequence.

Questions this article answers.

What are the 6 R's of cloud migration?

Rehost (lift and shift), replatform (lift and reshape), refactor (re-architect), repurchase (move to SaaS), retire (decommission), and retain (keep on-premises for now). Each workload is assigned one strategy based on its value, complexity, and constraints.

Is lift and shift a good cloud migration strategy?

Rehosting is fast and low-risk and is the right call for many workloads. But rehosting everything leaves cloud-native benefits on the table. The strongest strategies mix rehosting with selective replatforming and refactoring where the payoff is real.

How do I decide which strategy to use for each application?

Start with a portfolio assessment that scores each workload on business value, technical complexity, and constraints such as compliance and dependencies. High-value, high-change applications justify refactoring; stable commodity workloads favor rehosting or repurchasing; low-value applications are candidates to retire.
