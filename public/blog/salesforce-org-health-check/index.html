Salesforce Org Health Check: 10 Signs You Need an Expert Review

Your org rarely tells you it is in trouble. These ten signs do. Here is when a Salesforce health check pays for itself.

A Salesforce org health check is a structured review of your org's data, automation, security, architecture, and adoption to find the risks and inefficiencies that are quietly slowing you down. Salesforce problems rarely arrive as one dramatic failure. They accumulate: a broken flow here, a duplicate-riddled report there, a feature nobody uses. Individually easy to ignore, together they become the reason projects stall and users lose trust. These are the ten signs it is time for an expert review.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

You likely need a Salesforce org health check when you see recurring symptoms across four areas: unreliable data, brittle or unpredictable automation, security and access drift, and falling user adoption. A tenth and increasingly common trigger is a stalled AI or Agentforce initiative that cannot proceed because the underlying org is not ready. A health check examines data quality, automation, security model, technical debt, integration, and adoption, then returns a prioritized remediation plan. The goal is not a report - it is a ranked list of what to fix, in what order, and why.

What a Salesforce org health check actually is

It is a systematic assessment of the state of your org, conducted against known best practices and your own business goals. A good health check does not just list problems; it explains their business impact, ranks them by risk and effort, and recommends a sequence. It typically covers data quality, automation and code, the security and sharing model, technical debt, integrations, licensing and cost efficiency, and user adoption. The output is a decision tool for leadership, not a technical audit for its own sake.

Why it matters to the business

An unhealthy org is a slow, risky, and expensive org. It ships change more slowly, breaks more often, costs more to run, and blocks the initiatives that depend on it. Data is where this bites hardest: Gartner has estimated poor data quality costs organizations an average of $12.9 million per year , and in Salesforce that cost surfaces as wrong reports, misfired automation, and AI that cannot be trusted. As enterprises move to layer Agentforce and analytics onto their CRM, org health stops being hygiene and becomes the precondition for the roadmap.

The 10 signs you need an expert review

Any one of these is worth attention. Several together mean the interest on your org's accumulated problems is coming due.

1
Reports and dashboards contradict each other
Two dashboards answer the same question differently, and leadership no longer trusts the numbers. It signals data quality and definition problems beneath the reporting layer.
2
Automation breaks when you change something unrelated
A small edit triggers failures elsewhere. This is the classic tell of automation sprawl - overlapping flows and rules with no clear ownership.
3
Releases keep slowing down
Changes that used to take days now take weeks, and every deploy feels risky. It points to missing source control, weak testing, and accumulated technical debt.
4
Duplicate and incomplete records are everywhere
Sales and service teams waste time reconciling records, and campaigns hit the wrong contacts. A sign of missing deduplication and data governance.
5
Users are working around Salesforce
Teams keep parallel spreadsheets or skip steps. Low adoption usually means the org has drifted away from how people actually work.
6
Nobody can explain how a key process works
Critical automation exists, but no one fully understands it, and the person who built it has left. This is undocumented, high-risk complexity.
7
Your security and sharing model has drifted
Permissions have accreted over years, ex-employees may retain access, and nobody is confident who can see what. A compliance and risk exposure.
8
Integrations fail silently
Data stops syncing between Salesforce and other systems, and you find out from a customer, not an alert. It indicates fragile, unsupported integration.
9
Costs are rising without clear value
Licenses, storage, and add-ons grow, but the value is unclear and unused features abound. A sign of licensing and cost inefficiency worth auditing.
10
An AI or Agentforce project has stalled
You tried to launch an agent or advanced analytics and hit a wall. Almost always the org - its data and automation - was not ready for what you asked it to.

What an experienced reviewer examines

A credible health check goes beyond surface symptoms to root causes across these areas:

Area What is examined What it reveals
Data quality Duplicates, completeness, validation, definitions Whether the org can be trusted for reporting and AI
Automation & code Flows, Apex, test coverage, ownership Fragility and delivery risk

Technical debt Source control, release process, hardcoding How safely the org can change
Integration Sync reliability, monitoring, API governance Hidden failure points

Can we do a health check ourselves, or do we need an expert?

A strong internal admin team can and should monitor org health continuously, and for a contained, well-understood org, an internal review may be enough. An external expert earns their place in three situations: when you need an objective view that internal politics or familiarity can obscure, when the issues span data, architecture, and security in ways that need cross-domain experience to untangle, or when a health check is the first step before a major initiative like Agentforce, where being wrong about readiness is expensive. The value of an outside reviewer is pattern recognition: they have seen how these symptoms resolve across many orgs and can tell you which are cosmetic and which are load-bearing.

The cost of waiting

Ignoring the signs does not hold the problem steady - it lets it compound. Unmanaged automation grows more tangled, data drifts further, and more work gets built on the shaky foundation, making eventual remediation larger. The most expensive path is to discover the org's condition mid-way through a strategic project, when a stalled Agentforce launch or a failed integration forces an emergency review under deadline pressure. A planned health check on your own schedule is far cheaper than a forced one during a crisis.

Key takeaways

  • A Salesforce org health check is a structured review of data, automation, security, technical debt, integration, and adoption.
  • Watch for the ten signs - contradictory reports, brittle automation, slowing releases, duplicate data, workarounds, undocumented processes, security drift, silent integration failures, rising cost, and stalled AI.
  • The output should be a prioritized remediation plan, not just a list of problems.
  • Bring in an expert for objectivity, cross-domain issues, or readiness ahead of a major initiative like Agentforce.
  • Waiting compounds the cost; a planned review beats a forced one during a stalled project.

Questions this article answers.

What is a Salesforce org health check?

It is a structured review of your org's data quality, automation and code, security model, technical debt, integrations, cost, and user adoption, measured against best practices and your business goals. It returns a prioritized plan of what to fix and in what order.

How do I know if my Salesforce org needs a review?

Look for the warning signs: reports that contradict each other, automation that breaks unexpectedly, slowing releases, duplicate data, users working around the system, security drift, silent integration failures, rising cost, and stalled AI projects. One is worth attention; several together signal a review is overdue.

How often should you run a Salesforce health check?

Monitor core signals continuously, and run a thorough review at least annually or before any major initiative such as an Agentforce rollout, a large integration, or a big data migration. Orgs that change quickly benefit from more frequent checks.

Can we do a Salesforce health check internally?

A strong admin team can monitor health and review a contained org. Bring in an external expert when you need objectivity, when issues span data, architecture, and security together, or when the check is a readiness gate before a high-stakes initiative where being wrong is costly.

How does org health affect Agentforce readiness?

Directly. An AI agent acts on your data and through your automation, so an unhealthy org limits what an agent can safely do. Many stalled Agentforce projects trace back to an org that was not ready, which is why a health check is a sensible first step.
