Build vs. Buy AI Agents on Salesforce: A CIO's Decision Framework

"Build or buy" is the wrong question. The real one is how much of each - here's a clear framework to decide for your Salesforce use case.

Every CIO evaluating AI agents eventually hits the same fork: configure something native to the platform you already run, or build something custom that does exactly what you need and nothing you do not. Both answers are defensible. Both are also incomplete on their own - which is why the real decision usually is not build or buy, but how much of each.

Why "Build vs. Buy" Is the Wrong First Question

Framed as a binary, this decision produces binary mistakes: either an all-native implementation that hits a wall the moment business logic gets complex, or an all-custom build that reinvents infrastructure Salesforce already provides for free. The more useful framing has three options, not two.

Option 3: Hybrid - Agentforce Core With a Custom Integration Layer

🟢 Hybrid (Most Common Choice)

This is where most mid-market and enterprise teams land, and for good reason: use native Agentforce for the conversational interface, topic management, and platform-native actions, while building a custom integration and orchestration layer for the logic and systems that fall outside what the platform handles natively. You get the speed of native configuration for 70-80% of the use case, and the flexibility of custom development for the 20-30% that actually differentiates your business process.

Decision Matrix: Score Your Own Use Case

Rate each factor from 1 (favors native) to 5 (favors custom), then average:

  • Complexity of decision logic beyond simple rules

  • Number of non-Salesforce systems the agent must orchestrate across

  • Urgency of time-to-value versus tolerance for a longer build

  • Availability of internal engineering capacity for long-term ownership

  • Degree to which this agent's logic is a genuine competitive differentiator

READING YOUR SCORE
Averages below 2.5 typically point to native configuration. Scores above 3.5 point toward custom or hybrid. The middle ground is where a hybrid approach earns its added complexity.

Questions this article answers.

Should I build custom AI agents or configure Agentforce natively?

Native Agentforce configuration is faster to a first use case because it uses existing platform infrastructure. Custom-built agents take longer to stand up but offer more flexibility for logic outside native platform capabilities. Most mid-market enterprises land on a hybrid approach.

Can Agentforce integrate with custom-built systems and legacy applications?

Yes, through APIs, middleware, or an integration platform layer - though effort depends heavily on whether the legacy system exposes a modern API or needs a custom connector built first.

How do I decide between native configuration and custom agent development?

Score your use case against the complexity of its logic, integration requirements, time-to-value needs, and internal engineering capacity. Simple, platform-native workflows favor configuration; complex cross-system logic favors custom development or a hybrid model.
