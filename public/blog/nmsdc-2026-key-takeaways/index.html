NMSDC 2026, Oct 25 to 28, Los Angeles →

Four days, one convention center, and more handshakes than we could count. The 2026 NMSDC Annual Conference & Exchange took over the Los Angeles Convention Center from October 25 to 28, and our team was there for all of it. Here are our NMSDC 2026 key takeaways, straight from the floor.

This is our honest recap. What was on the official agenda, what we actually learned, the people who made the week worth the flight, and what we think procurement and supplier diversity leaders should do next. Where something comes from NMSDC, we link to it. Where it is our own reading, we say so.

NMSDC 2026 in 60 seconds

What it was: The National Minority Supplier Development Council's flagship event, held October 25 to 28, 2026 at the Los Angeles Convention Center under the theme "Drive Growth. Create Opportunity. Shape the Future of American Prosperity."

Our five biggest takeaways:

  • Certification gets you in the room. Readiness is what wins the contract.
  • The strongest growth stories were partnership stories, not solo bids.
  • AI is now part of supplier conversations, from vendor audits to how buyers find you.
  • Access to capital and global markets came up in session after session.
  • The best opportunities still started with a real conversation in a hallway.

What is the NMSDC Annual Conference and Exchange?

The NMSDC Annual Conference & Exchange is where certified minority business enterprises (MBEs) meet the corporate buyers, procurement teams and supplier diversity leaders who want to work with them. It mixes mainstage keynotes, hands-on Impact Labs, short Spark Sessions, a large exhibit hall called The Exchange, and pre-arranged 1:1 matchmaking.

The scale matters here. In its announcement for this year's event, NMSDC said its certified suppliers generate nearly $600 billion in economic output, support more than 2 million jobs and pay $168 billion in annual wages. That is the economy that showed up in Los Angeles.

For a company like ours, a certified minority- and woman-owned technology firm, it is one of the few rooms where enterprise buyers, government agencies and fellow MBEs are all looking for the same thing: partners they can trust to deliver.

NMSDC 2026 key takeaways: six lessons we brought home

These are our reading of the week, based on the sessions we attended and the conversations we had. Nobody handed us these on a slide.

1. Certification opens the door. Readiness wins the contract.

Almost every buyer we spoke with said some version of the same thing: being certified gets you considered, but it does not get you selected. The agenda leaned into this, with a Spark Session titled "Turning MBE Certification Into Contract Opportunities" and an Impact Lab from the U.S. Department of Transportation and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services called "Roadmap to Contract Readiness."

The gap between certified and contract-ready usually comes down to the basics. A tight capability statement. Clear proof of past performance. The right identifiers and codes ready to go. Security and compliance answers you can send the same day someone asks.

2. Partnerships beat going it alone

The session that stayed with us most was Power of Partnership: Uniting for Business Growth and Global Impact, an Impact Lab built around how organizations like the Export-Import Bank of the United States help small businesses compete in international markets. The speakers included Nada Allie of the Export-Import Bank of the United States, Terri Batch of Global LA, Pellson Lau of the U.S. Small Business Administration's Office of Manufacturing & Trade, and Simona Racek of Just Down the Street.

Their message was practical. Financing tools, trade programs and the right partners can take a lot of the risk out of growing beyond U.S. borders. The same idea showed up elsewhere on the agenda, in sessions like "Capacity Through Collaboration: How MBEs and Anchors Win as One Team." The pattern is hard to miss. Bigger contracts are increasingly won by teams, not by single suppliers.

Our honest reading: the MBEs growing fastest are not the ones chasing every RFP alone. They are the ones building a bench of partners they can bring into a bid with confidence.

3. AI is now part of the supplier conversation

AI was not a side topic this year. Monday's Impact Labs included "Leading in the AI Era: Five Decisions Every Executive Must Make Today," a session on how physical and IT security now shape vendor audits, and another on what your network, digital footprint and AI say about you before you even walk into a meeting. On Tuesday, the Technology Industry Group asked the direct question: is AI an opportunity or a disruption for supplier inclusion?

For buyers, that means sharper questions about how suppliers protect data and use AI responsibly. For suppliers, it means your security posture and your digital presence are part of your pitch now, whether you planned it or not.

4. Capital and global reach are growth levers, not afterthoughts

Access to capital came up again and again. The afternoon mainstage included "Accelerate Access to Capital: Driving Economic Impact & Empowering Communities," and NMSDC ran an invitation-only "Capital IS Culture LIVE" session through its Capital Readiness Program. Combine that with the export financing discussion in Power of Partnership, and the takeaway is simple. Growth plans that ignore funding and market access tend to stall at the first big contract.

5. Women-owned and minority-owned leadership was impossible to miss

Some of the most energizing conversations we had were with founders who have built serious businesses and now spend their time pulling others up behind them. As a certified minority- and woman-owned business ourselves, that matters to us. Representation is not a talking point at NMSDC. It is the whole room.

6. The hallway is still where deals start

Matchmaking sessions are valuable. But many of the most useful moments of the week happened between them, at the Welcome Reception under the aircraft at the California Science Center, at the kickoff wall, or over coffee before the morning sessions. Structured meetings get you time. Unstructured ones build trust.

The Power of Partnership panel

Impact Lab, October 28

Nada Allie, Terri Batch, Pellson Lau and Simona Racek gave one of the most practical sessions of the week on export financing, trade resources and growing beyond U.S. borders. Pellson Lau and Simona Racek also took the time to meet our team in person, and we are grateful for the conversation.

What procurement and supplier diversity leaders should do next

If you attended, the next two weeks decide whether the conversations turn into anything. If you missed it, the same steps still apply.

  • Follow up within ten days. Send a short note with one clear next step, not a brochure.
  • Ask for readiness, not just certification. Request capability statements, past performance and security documentation up front.
  • Look for teaming, not just vendors. Ask suppliers who they partner with and how they handle work that is bigger than they are.
  • Put AI and security on the first call. How a supplier protects your data now matters as much as what they build.
  • Track Tier 1 and Tier 2 spend from day one. It is far easier to report impact you measured from the start.
