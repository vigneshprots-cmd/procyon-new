Our view

Most custom code should have been configuration, or should not exist.

Enterprises that run Salesforce and ServiceNow often also run a long tail of custom applications: a quoting tool, a partner portal, a field app, an approvals system written fifteen years ago. Some of them do something the platforms cannot. Many duplicate what the platforms now do out of the box, and cost more every year to keep alive.

So engineering here starts with a portfolio question rather than a backlog. What should be retired, what should move onto Salesforce or ServiceNow, and what genuinely needs custom code? Only the last group gets a product team, and it is built to connect to the platforms rather than compete with them.

  • A custom app that copies the CRM

    A separate customer database, synced nightly, with its own version of every account. Two sources of truth and an integration to babysit.First move: read and write through Salesforce or ServiceNow APIs, not copies

  • Lift and shift that moved the bill

    Workloads were rehosted, the cloud invoice keeps growing, and releases are no faster than in the data center.First move: FinOps with an owner for every dollar, then replatform the costly few

  • The one developer who knows the code

    Production breaks at night and waits for the single person who understands it. Enhancements never get scheduled.First move: a support team with runbooks, monitoring and on-call cover

  • Security checked after release

    Misconfigurations and missing evidence turn every audit into a scramble, and the same findings return each year.First move: security checks and evidence collection in the delivery pipeline

Modernization paths

Six ways to modernize an application. Each one scored, not guessed.

Every application in scope is scored against all six, then moved in waves so the business never depends on one cutover. For many Salesforce and ServiceNow customers, repurchase and retire do more good than any rewrite.

Assess the portfolio →
Rehost
Lift and shift to the cloud as it is, for a quick exit from a data center. Rarely the end state.
Replatform
Small changes, such as a managed database, for better cost and resilience without a rewrite.
Refactor
Re-architect into cloud-native services where scale or release speed justifies the cost.
Repurchase
Replace with SaaS, such as moving a custom CRM to Salesforce or a home-grown help desk to ServiceNow.
Retire
Switch off what nobody uses, and stop paying to run and secure it.
Retain
Keep in place for now, with a date to revisit and a reason written down.

Asked before a build or modernization.

What does Procyon's product engineering include?

Custom web and mobile product engineering, application modernization, cloud transformation on AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, cybersecurity and compliance, and application maintenance and support, delivered by one team.

How is a legacy application modernized?

Each application is scored against six paths: rehost, replatform, refactor, repurchase, retire or retain. The portfolio is then moved in waves, so the business never depends on a single big-bang cutover.

Which clouds does Procyon work with?

AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, with secure landing zones, platform engineering and FinOps. Cloud optimization has reduced costs by 40% on average.

Can Procyon support applications after launch?

Yes. Application maintenance and support covers monitoring, fixes and enhancements across US, Canadian and Indian working hours.

Can custom applications connect to Salesforce and ServiceNow?

Yes. Products are built to use the CRM, ITSM and ERP systems the business already runs, through APIs and events rather than copied data.
