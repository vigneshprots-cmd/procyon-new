Our view

Most service desks have a data problem, not a tool problem.

When ITSM disappoints, the instinct is to redesign the forms or buy the next module. The cause is usually underneath: CIs that are duplicated or stale, services that were never modelled, and process designs copied from the tool the organization left.

Incident routing, change risk, event correlation and Now Assist all read the same CMDB. Fix it once and every one of them improves. Skip it and each new module inherits the errors.

  • Changes approved blind

    The change advisory board reviews every change because nobody trusts the impact analysis. Routine work queues for a weekly meeting, and the risky change still gets through.First move: change models with risk-based approval and a standard change catalog

  • Incidents routed by guesswork

    Assignment depends on a category the caller picked, not the service affected. Tickets bounce between groups, and repeat incidents never become problem records.First move: route on the affected service and its support group

  • Alert storms

    Monitoring tools send thousands of events with no CI attached. Operations cannot tell which alerts threaten a business service, so major incidents are declared late.First move: bind alerts to CIs, then correlate by service

  • A CMDB loaded, never maintained

    A one-time import filled the CMDB at go-live. Nothing keeps it current, so it drifts from reality within months.First move: Discovery schedules and an owner for every CI class

Why the CMDB comes first

Measured on three scores, owned by name.

ServiceNow's CMDB Health dashboard scores completeness, correctness and compliance. Those scores, and a named owner for each class, are the acceptance criteria for the CMDB work.

Start with the CMDB audit →
Completeness
Required and recommended attributes filled for each class, starting with the ones change and incident read.
Correctness
Duplicates merged, orphans resolved and stale CIs retired, so one server is one record.
Compliance
CIs audited against the expected configuration, with failures raised as tasks to the owner.
CSDM alignment
Business services, application services and technical services modelled the way the platform expects.
Identification and reconciliation
Every data source writes through identification rules, with clear precedence when two sources disagree.
Ownership
A named owner and support group for every CI class, and a health score they are accountable for.

How it runs

Data first, process second, cutover last.

Rollouts from another ITSM tool follow the same order. Processes are redesigned, not copied, and data is cleaned before it is migrated.

  1. Weeks 1 to 3Health check

    CMDB scores, Discovery coverage, current process design and the old tool's data assessed.

    Target model and scope signed off
  2. FoundationCMDB and Discovery

    MID Servers, Discovery schedules, identification rules and CSDM classes in place before any process depends on them.

    CMDB Health scores at the agreed level
  3. BuildProcesses

    Incident, problem, change and request configured and tested against real tickets from the last quarter.

    Process owners accept the design
  4. CutoverTrain and switch

    The service desk trained on its own scenarios, data migrated, and the old tool set to read-only.

    Go-live checklist signed
  5. After go-liveHypercare

    Handover to the run team

Asked before an ITSM or ITOM rollout.

Why should the CMDB be fixed before an ITSM rollout?

Incident routing, change impact, asset management and Now Assist all read the CMDB. If the CMDB is duplicated or stale, every one of those processes inherits the errors, so the CMDB is audited and reconciled first.

What is the difference between ITSM and ITOM?

ITSM covers how IT serves people: incidents, problems, changes, requests and assets. ITOM covers how IT runs infrastructure: Discovery, the CMDB, service mapping and event management. They share the same CMDB.

Can Procyon migrate from another ITSM tool?

Yes. Processes are redesigned around how the organization works, data is cleaned and migrated, and the service desk is trained before cutover.

What results has the ITSM and integration work delivered?

For a statewide judicial system, integration failures fell by 60% and root cause analysis became 40 to 50% faster. For Glendale Water and Power, 10+ systems were unified on one platform.
