Our view

Numbers disagree because definitions do, not because the warehouse is wrong.

When finance, sales and the board each bring a different revenue figure, the instinct is to buy a new platform. Usually the platform is fine. Bookings are counted from the Salesforce opportunity in one place and from the ERP invoice in another, and nobody ever wrote down which one is revenue.

That matters more now, because AI reads the same data. An agent or assistant grounded on two definitions of an active customer will give both answers, with confidence. Fix the definitions and the ownership first; the platform choice follows from the workloads.

  • Metrics defined in every dashboard

    Each Tableau or Power BI workbook carries its own calculation of revenue, churn or backlog. Twenty dashboards, five versions of the truth.First move: a governed semantic layer with one owner per metric

  • Platform chosen on brand

    Snowflake or Databricks picked because a peer did, before anyone listed the workloads. Costs climb and half the team works around it.First move: score the options on the actual workloads and skills

  • Pipelines that fail silently

    A source system changes a field overnight and the dashboard shows yesterday's numbers without saying so.First move: tested transformations and alerts when a source changes shape

  • AI on ungoverned data

    The AI team pulls straight from source systems because the platform has no product they can trust, and builds a second, private version of the customer.First move: publish governed data products that analytics and AI both use

Platform fit

Snowflake or Databricks is a fit question.

Both are capable, and many enterprises end up running both. The recommendation follows the workloads, the cloud already in place and the skills of the team that will run it.

Snowflake
Fits when the center of gravity is SQL analytics and governed reporting, with many business users, data shared across teams or partners, and a preference for a managed service with little platform engineering.
Databricks
Fits when data engineering and machine learning dominate: large pipelines, semi-structured and unstructured data, notebooks, and a team comfortable with Spark and Python.
BigQuery
Fits estates already on Google Cloud and Google Workspace, where analytics and Vertex AI sit side by side.
Synapse
Fits estates standardized on Azure and Microsoft identity, with Power BI as the main reporting tool.

Whichever platform is chosen, the semantic layer and the data products are designed to outlive it, and to feed Salesforce Data Cloud and ServiceNow reporting from the same governed source.

Asked before modernizing a data platform.

Which data platform should we standardize on?

Procyon works across Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery and Synapse. The right choice depends on workloads, existing investments and ecosystem, so the options are scored on total cost of ownership, performance, governance and team fit before a recommendation is made.

Is a semantic layer still needed with modern BI tools?

Yes, for any organization that wants one definition per metric. A governed semantic layer keeps KPIs consistent across Tableau, Power BI, Looker and AI tools, so teams stop arguing about whose number is right.

How is data governance and privacy handled?

Through a catalog, lineage, quality rules and access controls designed into the platform, aligned with GDPR, CCPA and HIPAA, so sensitive data is governed before it reaches dashboards or AI.

Can the data platform feed AI and automation?

Yes. Trusted data products are built to serve dashboards, AI agents, knowledge assistants and Salesforce Data Cloud from the same governed source.

What results do data platform programs deliver?

Across more than 100 data platforms delivered, reporting has become up to 80% faster and self-service adoption has grown up to tenfold. For Glendale Water & Power, 10+ utility systems were unified with a 15-day megawatt forecasting pipeline.
