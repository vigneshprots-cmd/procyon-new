Our view

Bad placements are decided at the CV, not at the rate card.

The expensive part of a wrong hire is not the hourly rate. It is the weeks before anyone admits the person cannot do the work, the sprint the team loses covering for them, and the weeks it then takes to replace them.

So the effort goes in at the front: a role defined by what the person must deliver, a technical screen run by practitioners rather than recruiters, and references that confirm the work on the CV actually happened.

  • Keyword-matched CVs

    The certifications are listed and the platform names are right, but nobody has checked whether the person has built anything with them.First move: a hands-on screen against the role scorecard

  • A role written as a title

    "Salesforce developer" can mean Apex, Lightning components, integration or Revenue Cloud configuration. A shortlist cannot be better than the request.First move: a scorecard with skills, seniority and success criteria on day 0

  • Seniority measured in years

    Ten years of the same release cycle is not the same as ten years of judgement under delivery pressure.First move: a scenario interview built from real program situations

  • No way out when it is wrong

    Contracts that make replacement a negotiation keep the wrong person in the seat for weeks longer than anyone wants.First move: a no-questions-asked replacement guarantee in the contract

Vetting

Four checks before a profile reaches the shortlist.

Unverified CVs are the biggest risk in staffing. Every candidate passes all four before a client sees the name, and if a placement still is not the right fit, a replacement is found. No debate.

Request a shortlist →
  • Technical screenA hands-on assessment of the platform skills the role scorecard asks for, run by practitioners from the matching practice.
  • Scenario interviewReal program situations, not trivia, to test judgement under delivery pressure: a failed deployment, a conflicting requirement, a data migration gone wrong.
  • Communication checkClear written and spoken English for client stand-ups, stakeholder calls and the tickets other people will have to read.
  • Reference checksPast clients and managers confirm that the work on the CV actually happened, and at the level described.

How fast can Procyon place a Salesforce or ServiceNow consultant?

A shortlist of vetted candidates arrives within 72 hours for most roles, and the average time from request to an onboarded consultant is 7 to 14 days. Niche, cleared or highly specialized roles can take longer, with the timeline agreed upfront.

How are candidates vetted?

Every candidate passes a technical screen, a scenario-based interview, a communication assessment and reference checks. Placements are backed by a no-questions-asked replacement guarantee.

Which engagement models are available for staff augmentation?

Time and materials, fixed-price pods, milestone-based and managed capacity, with onshore, nearshore (Canada) and offshore (India) blends at predictable rates.

Can the team scale up or down quickly?

Yes. The bench and active sourcing networks allow teams to scale up or down within two weeks. Most clients keep a steady core team plus a flex layer that follows demand.

Is government-cleared talent available?

Yes. Procyon maintains a pool of US-cleared resources for federal, state and local engagements, including FedRAMP-aligned delivery and public-trust roles.

Which skills can Procyon supply?

Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP, Workday and Oracle specialists; AWS, Azure, GCP, Snowflake, Databricks and AI engineers; full-stack, mobile, DevOps and QA automation engineers; and architects, project managers, business analysts and scrum masters.
