Our view

Seats add hours. A pod adds an owner.

When a backlog outgrows the in-house team, the reflex is to add contractors one seat at a time. Each one needs direction, review and context from the same overloaded lead, so throughput rises far less than headcount.

A pod arrives with the roles that usually become the bottleneck already inside it: design authority, someone writing the stories, and someone testing them. The client sets priorities and accepts the work; the pod owns getting it done.

  • The lead becomes the bottleneck

    Every design question and every code review queues behind one in-house architect, who stops doing architecture.First move: an architect inside the pod with design authority

  • Stories arrive half-written

    Developers build what the ticket says, the business meant something else, and the sprint ends in rework.First move: a business analyst who owns story quality

  • Testing left to the end

    QA joins in the last sprint before release, finds the defects that compound, and the date moves.First move: QA in the pod from sprint one

  • Accountability nobody signed

    Seats bill for hours whether or not the sprint lands. Nobody outside the client team is on the hook for the result.First move: sprint outcomes in the contract, on fixed-price or milestone terms

Pod shapes

Six starting shapes, each sized to a different roadmap.

Every pod is sized to the backlog. These are the shapes delivery leads adjust from.

Salesforce build pod
Architect, developers, admin, BA and QA. New clouds, features, automation and Agentforce agents, sprint by sprint. Best when the Salesforce backlog is bigger than the in-house team.
ServiceNow pod
Architect, developers, BA and QA for ITSM, ITOM, CMDB, Security Operations and GRC roadmaps, plus tech-debt removal. Best when the instance needs steady change without a long hiring cycle.
AI and data pod
Architect with AI, integration and data engineers. Agents, knowledge assistants, MuleSoft integration and Data Cloud. Best when AI use cases are queued but the data work is not staffed.
Product engineering pod
Full-stack, mobile, DevOps and QA automation engineers for custom applications, modernization and release pipelines around the platform.
Run pod
Admins, developers and QA on managed capacity for enhancements, releases and support after go-live, with shared accountability.
Custom pod
Platform specialists, engineers and project leadership mixed into one squad, for a roadmap that crosses more than one platform.

Asked before starting a delivery pod.

What is a dedicated delivery pod?

A delivery pod is a cross-functional team, typically an architect, developers, a business analyst and QA, that works as one squad on a client roadmap with shared accountability for sprint outcomes, rather than individual people billed by the hour.

How is a pod different from staff augmentation?

With staff augmentation, people join the client team and the client directs the work. A delivery pod owns the sprint outcomes as a team, arrives with all the roles it needs and can be contracted on fixed-price or milestone terms.

How quickly can a pod scale?

Capacity can be added or released within two weeks. Most clients keep a steady core team plus a flex layer that follows demand.

Which commercial terms are available for pods?

Time and materials, fixed-price pods, milestone-based and managed capacity, with onshore, nearshore (Canada) and offshore (India) blends at predictable rates.

Which platforms can a pod work on?

Salesforce, ServiceNow, AI and data including MuleSoft and Data Cloud, and custom product engineering. Pods can also mix platforms when a roadmap crosses more than one.
