Our view

Security operations on ServiceNow succeed or fail in the CMDB.

Vulnerability Response is only as good as its match rate. A finding that lands on a CI with an owner and a support group becomes a remediation task. A finding that matches nothing goes to a default queue and waits.

GRC has the same dependency. Continuous control monitoring needs systems to read evidence from and owners to attest. When the CMDB and the control library are weak, the security team is back to spreadsheets, just inside ServiceNow.

  • Patching ranked by CVSS alone

    Every critical looks equally urgent, so the team patches what is easy. Nobody can say which vulnerable servers support a business service.First move: risk scoring that weighs the business service and exposure of each CI

  • Findings with nowhere to go

    Scanner imports create vulnerable items that match no CI, or match a CI with no owner. The backlog grows on paper, not on anyone's queue.First move: measure the CI match rate, then close the gaps with Discovery

  • Incidents run in email

    Alerts reach the SOC from several tools, analysts enrich them by hand and escalations travel as forwarded threads with no record of who did what.First move: Security Incident Response playbooks for the top three incident types

  • Controls tested once a year

    Policies live in documents, risks in spreadsheets and evidence in inboxes. Controls drift between audits and nobody notices until fieldwork starts.First move: one control library mapped to every framework in force
