Our view

A certificate answers one question. Know which one.

Vendor onboarding tends to collect credentials as a checklist. Each one answers a different risk, and none of them answers all of it.

A partner badge describes the firm, not the people on the program. A security certificate describes a managed system, not one project's controls. The useful question is what each credential proves, and what to ask for alongside it.

  • Certified people, or a certified proposal?

    Partner status says the firm meets the vendor's program requirements. It says nothing about who is assigned to the work.Ask for: the named team and the certifications each person holds

  • Managed security, or a questionnaire?

    A completed questionnaire is a description. ISO 27001 means an external auditor has checked that the management system exists and is followed.Ask for: the certificate, and the scope statement printed on it

  • A process, or the right people on a good day?

    Delivery quality that depends on who gets assigned does not survive staff changes. ISO 9001 is about the process that remains when people move.Ask for: the phase sign-offs written into the statement of work

  • Patient data handled, or just stored?

    There is no official HIPAA certification. What matters is how protected health information is handled during build, testing and support.Ask for: how PHI is kept out of sandboxes and who can see it

ISO/IEC 27001

The international standard for an information security management system: risks assessed, controls chosen against them, and the system checked by an external auditor.

Controls in deliveryAccess to client environments, asset handling, supplier management and incident response run under the audited system, which shortens vendor security reviews.

Quality

ISO 9001:2015

The international standard for a quality management system: documented processes, measured results and corrective action when something goes wrong.

Repeatable deliveryDocumented delivery processes with sign-off at each phase, and corrective actions tracked to closure rather than handled case by case.
