Our view

Rollouts are lost in discovery, long before build.

The most common way to waste a Salesforce budget is to rebuild the old system with a better logo. Every spreadsheet, approval email and four-step handoff comes across, and a year later the licence bill is higher and nothing moves faster.

So the first weeks go on the process, not the org: which stages a deal really passes through, who owns a case at each point, what a sales manager needs to see on a Monday. Configuration follows the agreed process. Anything that cannot justify itself is cut before it is built.

  • Stages nobody defined

    Pipeline stages without entry and exit criteria turn the forecast into a measure of rep optimism. The CFO stops trusting it within a quarter.First move: exit criteria per stage, signed by sales leadership

  • Sharing designed last

    When the role hierarchy and sharing model are left until UAT, the fix is a pile of sharing rules that slows every save and nobody can audit.First move: the sharing model in the blueprint, before any object is built

  • Migrating everything

    Every record since the old system went live is loaded, because no one decided what to leave behind. Old duplicates and dead accounts dilute every report from day one.First move: a keep, archive or drop decision for each object

Risk register

Five reasons rollouts stall, and the control for each.

Most of the rescue work Procyon is asked to do traces back to one of these. Each one has a control written into the plan before build starts.

Get a rollout risk review →
The failureWhat it looks likeThe control

DiscoveryThe org copies the old process

Every spreadsheet and workaround is rebuilt in Salesforce, so nothing gets faster and users wonder what the project was for.

Process redesign before configurationDiscovery maps the target process first. Each requirement is traced to a decision it supports, and anything that does not earn its place is cut.

DataDirty data on day one

Duplicates and orphaned records migrate in, and users stop trusting the reports within a week.

Cleanse, dedupe and reconcileData is cleaned and mapped during build, trial-loaded into a sandbox, and every production load ends with a reconciliation report on record counts, key fields and owners.

AutomationAutomation sprawl

Flows, triggers and legacy rules collide on the same object, so records save slowly or fail with errors nobody can explain.

One automation pattern per objectAn architect owns the automation design. Flow is the default, Apex only where volume demands it, never both firing on the same save, and every flow is documented before go-live.

IntegrationIntegrations tested last

ERP and billing sync break under real volume a week after launch, and orders stop reaching finance.

Integration in the build, not after itInterfaces are built alongside configuration and load-tested at production volumes against API limits, with MuleSoft where it fits and error queues someone owns.

AdoptionUsers never adopt it

Reps keep the old spreadsheet, managers run the forecast call from it, and the licence spend shows no return.

Role-based enablement and hypercareTraining by role on the user's own records, champions in each team, and adoption dashboards watched for 30 days after launch.

Commercials

Priced per phase, so risk stays visible.

The rollout plan recommends a model for each phase. Most programs combine a fixed-scope starter with a more flexible phase after it.

  • Fixed-scope starterSales Cloud Quick-Start or Service Cloud Rapid Deploy, with deliverables, timeline and cost fixed up front.
  • Fixed price per phaseLarger programs priced one phase at a time, each against a signed scope and a gate.
  • Time and materialsFor roadmaps that will change, such as a second cloud or AI use cases still being explored.
  • Outcome-linkedPart of the fee tied to a business result agreed in writing, such as adoption or response time.

Asked before every Salesforce rollout.

How long does a Salesforce implementation take?

With Procyon's fixed-scope starters, Sales Cloud goes live in 6 weeks and Service Cloud in 8 weeks. Multi-cloud programs run in phases, and each phase puts a working capability in front of users. Accelerators typically cut timelines by 30 to 50%.

What does a Salesforce implementation include?

Discovery, solution design, configuration, data migration, integration, testing, training and hypercare. You also receive a solution blueprint, an automation register, reconciled data and test evidence, so your own admins can run the org.

Can you migrate data from HubSpot, Dynamics or another CRM?

Yes. Legacy data is cleansed, deduplicated and mapped during the build phase, then loaded with reconciliation reports that prove record counts and key fields match the source.

Do you integrate Salesforce with our ERP?

Yes. We integrate Salesforce with SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Workday and other systems, using MuleSoft or native APIs, and load-test the interfaces before go-live rather than after it.

Is a fixed-price Salesforce implementation possible?

Yes. Starters such as Sales Cloud Quick-Start and Service Cloud Rapid Deploy are fixed scope. Larger programs can be fixed price per phase, time and materials, or partly tied to business outcomes.

What happens after go-live?

A hypercare period with adoption tracking, followed by an optional managed services retainer for admin work, Salesforce release management and continuous improvement.
