Our view

Most Salesforce problems are design debt, not product gaps.

When an org disappoints, the usual answer is another cloud or another add-on. In the orgs we audit, the cause is older and cheaper to fix: automation layered on automation, a data model bent around one team's spreadsheet, and integrations that nobody owns.

Agentforce raises the stakes. An agent reads the same records the reps read, so a duplicate account or a stale stage stops being an annoyance and becomes a wrong answer given to a customer.

  • Automation that fights itself

    Workflow rules, Process Builder, flows and Apex triggers all writing the same fields. Support for Workflow Rules and Process Builder ended in 2025, so the overlap is now a risk as well as a slowdown.First move: one automation pattern per object, migrated to Flow

  • CPQ on borrowed time

    Salesforce CPQ is no longer sold to new customers and new investment is going into Revenue Cloud. Quote errors and slow approvals rarely justify a like-for-like rebuild; they justify a cleaner pricing model.First move: a pricing rule audit before any migration plan

  • A data model nobody would design today

    Custom objects that duplicate standard ones, hundreds of fields on Opportunity, sharing rules added one escalation at a time. Reports disagree because the model does.First move: field usage audit and a target data model

  • Agents ahead of the data

    Agentforce pilots stall when the grounding data has duplicates, missing owners or unclear consent. The fix is upstream, in Data Cloud identity rules and data quality, not in the prompt.First move: score the data before choosing the first agent

Commercials

Four ways to contract, picked to fit the scope.

The first call ends with a written recommendation on which model fits the work and the procurement process. Most programs mix two.

  • Fixed scopeDefined deliverables, timeline and cost. Best when the outcome is clear, such as a cloud rollout or a starter.
  • Time and materialsFlexible capacity for a roadmap that will change, or for AI use cases still being explored.
  • Outcome-basedPart of the fee tied to a business KPI, such as response time or win rate, agreed in writing up front.
  • Managed servicesA monthly retainer for admin, releases and improvement once the org is live.

Asked before every Salesforce engagement.

What does a Salesforce consulting partner do?

A Salesforce consulting partner designs, implements, integrates and supports Salesforce for a business. Procyon covers the full cycle: implementation of Sales, Service, Revenue, Experience and Data Cloud, Agentforce AI agents, org audits and rescue, integration with ERP and other systems, and managed services after go-live.

How long does a Salesforce implementation take?

Fixed-scope starters are the fastest route: Sales Cloud goes live in 6 weeks and Service Cloud in 8 weeks. Multi-cloud programs are phased so each phase ships something usable. Procyon's 40+ accelerators typically shorten timelines by 30 to 50% compared with building from scratch.

Can Procyon fix a Salesforce org another partner built?

Yes. Rescue work is a large share of the Salesforce practice. It starts with an org health check across eight areas, ranks every finding by business impact, then stabilizes the org before rebuilding anything that blocks adoption.

Does Procyon implement Agentforce?

Yes. The Agent Launch Kit puts pre-configured Agentforce agents for lead qualification, case triage or self-service into production in under four weeks, grounded in company data and governed with human-in-the-loop controls.

Which Salesforce certifications does the team hold?

Procyon is a Salesforce Consulting Partner. The team holds Administrator, Platform Developer, Sales Cloud Consultant, Service Cloud Consultant, Revenue Cloud, Data Cloud and Einstein AI certifications.

How are Salesforce projects priced?

Procyon offers fixed-scope projects, time and materials, outcome-based contracts tied to business KPIs, and managed services retainers. After a 30-minute call, a senior architect sends a written plan with scope, timeline and a cost range.
